# Gold techniques (G01–G08)

Learned from the best lines the user hand-picked. The scene writer uses them as top-quality references.

## Rules
- Quality bar: the user writes each scene idea in about four versions and shares the 2–3 strongest lines from each. Those lines set the minimum level for punch strength, timing and creativity; every new scene must match or beat them with fresh lines. Each new set is studied and added here as new gold techniques (G09, G10, …).
- Use only ONE or TWO gold techniques per scene, the ones that fit the situation naturally.
- Never copy the original lines; keep only the technique.
- Slang follows the scene and region the user asks for (Telangana → authentic Telangana; Rayalaseema, Uttarandhra, etc. likewise). Don't mix regions unless the scene calls for it.
- Quality bar for every scene: powerful punch dialogues, natural character comedy, non-stop humour, twists, strong timing, character-to-character punches, trailer-level moments and an explosive final punchline.

## G01 · Three-place logic ladder (మూడో చోటు ఏమనుకుంటారు?)

**Best voice:** Sidekick

**How it works:** Give two places and what people assume about someone hiding there (normal, believable), then ask about the third, shameful place you're actually in. The first two build a rule; the third breaks it and hands your partner the set-up.

**Timing:** Whispered, slow on the first two, a pause, then the third place said flat.

## G02 · Knowledge went to the wrong body part (బుర్రకి కాదు, పొట్టకి)

**Best voice:** Lead

**How it works:** Explain fear or failure as a lesson that travelled to the wrong organ (not the brain, straight to the stomach/legs), so cowardice becomes the body's honest 'answer paper'. Close with a made-up English '-ness' tag.

**Timing:** Said proudly, like a scientific finding; the English tag is the button.

## G03 · Veteran's timetable with a dignified day off (ఫస్ట్ టైమా? ఇది నా పర్మనెంట్ అడ్రస్)

**Best voice:** Lead

**How it works:** A friend comforts the hero as a first-timer. The hero reveals it's his 'permanent address' since childhood, recites a weekday timetable of the same shameful act, adds one day off for a petty self-respect reason, then reframes his visible symptom as rank ('my legs shake from seniority, not fear').

**Timing:** Fast rhythm for the timetable (— ఇక్కడే ×3), a pause before the day off, the reframe last.

## G04 · The boss isn't human — cosmic simile (సూర్యుడు నైట్ షిఫ్ట్‌కి వచ్చినట్టు)

**Best voice:** Lead

**How it works:** Dismiss the danger by insisting the authority never has ordinary human needs, personify his discipline in a silly costume, then cap it with an impossible cosmic image ('fearing that is like fearing the sun on night shift').

**Timing:** Build two 'facts' about the boss, then the cosmic simile as the punch — just before the boss appears.

## G05 · Humiliation told as a proud legend (బిస్కెట్ ఖైదీ)

**Best voice:** Lead

**How it works:** Asked about shame, tell a vivid past humiliation with one concrete everyday object as the rescue, ending with the nickname people still use for him — told proudly, with an English tag.

**Timing:** The object (a biscuit packet) is the laugh; the nickname is the second laugh; the tag closes it.

## G06 · Overheard sincerity → one clue reveals the boss (ఒక చెప్పు నీలం, ఒకటి నలుపు)

**Best voice:** Sidekick → Lead

**How it works:** One friend admires a hidden 'sincere kid' from what he overhears (studying even in pain). The other spots one odd physical clue that only one person has, and reveals it's the authority — hiding from his own problem.

**Timing:** Let the admiration land first; the clue line is quick and sharp; the reveal is a shout.

**Timing:** Always the last words of the speech, said with a small finger-point.
